Untethered Health Monitor

When Earth can't answer, the crew still can.

A health monitoring system for crews far from Earth. It watches each astronaut's body, the cabin air and the radiation around them; turns an abnormal reading into a clear task for the right person; and keeps checking until the data shows recovery — on board, even when advice from Earth is minutes away or cut off. Everything runs in your browser: no install, no login.

How it works: one pipeline for every health signal

  1. MeasureEvery source of health data on board: wearables, brain sensing, the medical kit, cabin sensors and the crew's own reports.
  2. Trust the dataEvery reading carries its source, quality and age. Bad or missing data is measured again, never mistaken for good health.
  3. DetectReadings are judged against NASA standards and medical evidence, and every judgement shows where it comes from.
  4. AnticipateForecasts of the cabin and of each person's risk bring checks forward, before symptoms appear.
  5. ActEach alert becomes one coordinated plan: every crew member gets their own task and knows why it is theirs.
  6. Confirm and reportA problem closes only when the data shows recovery. Earth is kept informed, and late advice is weighed against the present.

The system is open to new measurements: a glucose meter, a new wearable or a new cabin sensor joins the same pipeline as a sensor, a rule and a procedure — no new code.

Prediction: seeing problems before they arrive

HealthEach astronaut's own baseline, combined with evidence on how CO₂, radiation and lost sleep affect the body, estimates who may be affected and when.
Act early, keep scoreChecks are brought forward to the moment they matter, and every forecast is scored against what really happened, so the crew knows how far to trust it.

Alarms and data: the right information to the right person

  1. On the wristAn alert finds the person wherever they are, and keeps reminding until it is acknowledged.
  2. In the handEach crew member sees their own readings, what they mean, and exactly what to do next.
  3. Around the cabinShip-wide hazards are seen and heard by everyone, graded by urgency. Personal health stays private.
  4. Crew medical officerHas the whole crew's picture and makes the medical decisions on board.
  5. EarthSees what the ship has reported and how old it is, and advises across the delay.

Many crew, one plan: coordinating the response

  • The right person for each task, chosen by role, skill, location and health. Someone who is unwell is relieved of risky work.
  • Nobody alone. Crewmates check on each other, and hazardous work is always watched.
  • First things first: protect people, check, fix the source, treat, and measure again.
  • The plan adapts. When something more urgent happens, work pauses and resumes on its own afterwards.
  • Every assignment explains itself, and any task can be handed back and re-planned.

Any outpost: spacecraft, rover or surface base

  • The same system travels. A spacecraft, a pressurized rover and a surface base each run it with their own crew, sensors and procedures.
  • Split crews stay coordinated. When the rover and the base lose contact, each side keeps working on what it knows and catches up when the link returns.
  • Alarms follow the person, from the cabin to the wrist to the helmet during a spacewalk.
  • Fewer people, adapted rules. Procedures scale to a two-person crew, with medical support from the base.
  • Real radiation NASA data. Curiosity's measured May 2024 solar storm tells the pair to end the EVA and return to the rover.
